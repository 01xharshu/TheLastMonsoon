# Cantonment service buildings — interior and exterior pass

Updated 2026-10-05 IST. These are original fictional 1857 environment candidates, integrated in the normal Suryagarh world. Detailed art and historical approval remain open.

- Grain/fodder warehouse: raised slatted dunnage, supported sacks in two rows, bound fodder bundles and issue basket; central loading aisle retained. Rear openings have sill stones, jambs, iron bars and open shutters.
- Cavalry stable: eight straw-covered stalls, rail supports, feed surfaces and water buckets, plus a recessed-looking channel between low drain lips. It is a visual drainage candidate; fluid drainage and stable husbandry are not simulated. Stable moved two metres north to separate its eaves from the chapel.
- Military hospital: eight cots with headboards, bedside cabinets and small water vessels, dressing basin, cloth rolls and medicine shelving. Persistent hospital bandage retained. Rear openings improve ward daylight; nursing and treatment remain unimplemented.
- Cantonment church: new twelve-by-fourteen-metre chapel at local (0,0,-33), ten pews, centre aisle, raised chancel, linen-covered altar, cross and cast bell in an open bell frame. Bell and services are scenery. This position preserves the eastern cantonment approach.
- Military cemetery: new walled burial ground at local (-61,0,0), open gate, central path, ten grave plots with stone bases and mixed cross/plain markers. No invented names or dates, and no exterior building lettering.

The four enclosed service buildings receive correctly oriented timber roof ties, principal rafters and king posts, plus a dedicated rough slab-floor shader. The cemetery uses the existing surveyed terrain, without a second collision ground sheet. Details are merged through the existing material batching system; moving medical supplies retain their separate geometry.

First native Metal review exposed church/stable roof overlap, incorrectly oriented roof ties, implausible sack stacking and oversized ward pots. Those were corrected before the final review run. Exterior review also exposed the last stable bay extending through the end wall; the eight-bay row was shifted to fit within the shell. Twelve entrance walks, cemetery gate walk, 35 resident terrain samples and three magazine vents passed on native Forward+/Metal. Final run: `/tmp/tlm_service_verified_metal.log`; refreshed evidence uses `captures/service_*.png`. Current result is recorded in `cantonment_validation.json`. Validation is mechanical route evidence, separate from visual acceptance.

Period basis: [Meerut heritage's garrison church history](https://heritagemeerut.in/Eng/StJohnChurch.html) supports the presence of Company garrison churches before 1857. [British Library's Meerut burial records, 1845–1854](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002295024) support period burial grounds, but are Roman Catholic records, not evidence for this exact Anglican layout. [National Army Museum cavalry roles](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/cavalry-roles) supports military horse use. These sources establish institution types, not the exact architecture or furnishings. Later pavilion hospital and First World War stable references were excluded as exact 1857 design authority.

Open art limits: architecture and grave silhouettes remain simple; fodder bundles, bed linen and plaster need richer geometry/material relief and hand-authored wear. Consecration orientation, exact bell/church form, hospital sanitary separation and stall fittings require period-specific research. Horse/staff population, interior night lighting, close animation contact and 8 GB performance are still open. Do not treat route PASS as realism approval.
