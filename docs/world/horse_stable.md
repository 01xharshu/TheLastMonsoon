# Village horse and stable, 1856–1859

The playable Bhairavpur stable is at world coordinates (-260, 194). It contains one bay horse, a timber and earth shelter with thatch roof, feed and water troughs, and plain leather tack. Arjun can take the horse with F, ride with W/S, steer with A/D, gallop with Shift, jump with Space, and dismount with F. The horse's `stolen` state becomes true when taken.

The material and tack choices are restrained to avoid modern equipment. Horse supply and cavalry use in 1857 are supported by [British Library India Office stud records](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/?f%5Bparent_reference_ssi%5D%5B%5D=IOR%2FL%2FMIL%2F5%2F459-467&f%5Burl_non_blank_si%5D%5B%5D=No&sort=hierarchy), [the July 1857 parliamentary discussion of cavalry horses](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1857/jul/13/the-mutiny-in-india-question), and an [1859 sketch of an Indian cavalryman](https://collection.nam.ac.uk/detail.php?acc=2002-04-909-18) in the National Army Museum collection. These establish the era context; they do not verify this particular stable architecture, horse breed, or exact saddle pattern.

The body and head now use an original continuous mesh. Editable source: `WorkingAssets/Horse/village_horse_body.blend` (SHA-256 `73e3c6397f2761d12344e2ef32eb00ab0dd367fc3ac568eb0065c0df64e66584`); reproducible builder: `tools/horses/build_horse_body.py`; runtime: `assets/animals/horse/village_horse_body.glb` (SHA-256 `15137e179e1a28d4b86b1e29d3c70aead82da54d87277cf9205e8e56510109a9`). It is original procedural modeling, not a documented historical breed study.

Validation: `tools/world/validate_horse_stable.gd` checks spawn, take/theft flag, measured hand/rein and foot/stirrup alignment, riding out of the stable entrance, jump, dismount, and earth/road/timber ground selection in the loaded world. `tools/horses/capture_horse_review.gd` generated Forward+/Metal standing, walking, and jumping frames: `docs/world/captures/19_horse_rider_side.png`, `21_horse_walk.png`, and `22_horse_jump.png`. The horse has two-segment legs, walk and faster gait phases, limited gallop stamina, reins, stirrups, and saddle front/back. The stable has rafters, roof courses, and open feed/water troughs. The current riding pose bends Arjun's knees forward, keeps boots near the stirrup treads, lowers both hands toward the reins, and adds a small pace/airborne lean. The tack now has a rounded saddle profile and visible side/under girth. The gait now makes a small per-leg height correction to keep planted hooves near the ground and lift advancing hooves; the focused ride check bounds their measured clearance. Fresh Forward+/Metal side, walk, and jump captures were regenerated after this change. The horse still looks stiff in the walking frame, and horse anatomy, leg articulation, hoof grounding, jump arc, tail, tack construction, and rider contact through full motion remain visibly simplified. Hand/foot bone positions near tack anchors are a contact measurement, not proof that palms grip reins or boots bear weight in stirrups throughout the gait. Theft marks the horse and displays a message; no witness, pursuit, ownership persistence, or crime response is implemented.

Sound pass: `audio/horses/` contains two alternating earth hoofbeats, packed-road and timber-bridge hoofbeats, a landing impact, leather movement, and an intermittent snort generated from the project's own script, plus a public-domain horse neigh recording. See `audio/horses/README.md` for exact files, provenance, and hashes. Spatial players fire from the horse on gait contacts, mounting/dismounting, takeoff, landing, first theft, and idle breaths. The focused ride check asserts hoof and landing triggers and road/bridge selection; final loudness and naturalness against music still need a listening review.
