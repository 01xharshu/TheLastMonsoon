# Horse cart candidates — 1856–1859

Two isolated Godot/Metal candidates are in `vehicles/horse_cart_candidate.gd`: a two-wheeled passenger **ekka** with bench and footboard, and a two-wheeled open produce cart with deeper side boards and a tied load. Each has wooden spoke wheels with dark metal tyres, an axle, two draft shafts, leather traces, reins, and one rigged horse. `set_forward_motion(speed, delta)` turns both wheels and switches the draft horse between idle and walk. They are **not yet placed in the live world** or boardable/driveable.

## British household family carriage candidate

`vehicles/family_carriage_candidate.gd` adds a distinct covered **four-wheel, two-horse carriage** for a family travelling with a separate coachman. It has a passenger cabin with two opposing benches (four seat sockets), side doors and steps, an exterior coachman's box and seat socket, paired draft horses, a central pole, traces, front and rear axles, and wheels that rotate at their different radii. The coachman figure is a **simple posture blockout**. No approved British family or driver character is seated yet; there is no boarding, driving, traffic, collision, or in-world placement.

The closest region-and-period transport evidence is the [British Library record of a four-wheel, two-horse *karanchy* in Calcutta, c.1850](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/?f%5Brelated_subjects_ssim%5D%5B%5D=Road+transport&f%5Burl_non_blank_si%5D%5B%5D=No&page=1&per_page=50&sort=date). An [1830–1835 Calcutta coachman study](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-003277883) supports a separate coachman role and period livery. A [British Museum 19th-century Delhi painting](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_1920-0917-0-252) shows a horse-drawn carriage approaching Ludlow Castle with a passenger and groom. These support the concept, not the exact cabin proportions, paint, suspension, harness layout, family count, or use at this game's fictional residence. The c.1850 source describes a modest vehicle; this candidate's enclosed household body is an interpretation and should not be called an exact *karanchy* reconstruction.

`tools/horses/validate_family_carriage_candidate.gd` checks four wheels, two rigged horses, distinct coachman/family seats, cabin and door parts, draft gear, and wheel/horse walk response. `tools/horses/capture_family_carriage_candidate.gd` produced [three-quarter](captures/family_carriage_quarter.png), [side](captures/family_carriage_side.png), and [front](captures/family_carriage_front.png) Forward+/Metal views. Inspected frames show a recognizable covered carriage, but angular bodywork, open window gaps, rudimentary suspension, a mannequin coachman, and visually unproven anatomical harness fit. Next: refine the body and harness from closer primary imagery, fit an approved driver/family to the seat sockets, then perform a bounded live-world road/turning test before gameplay use.

2026-09-24 detail pass: the cabin now has individually defined seat cushions, upholstered back panels, a floor mat, ceiling lining and interior door pulls. Outside, inset door panels, hinges, a second boarding step with brackets, paint mouldings, transparent side/front glazing, suspension shackles, roof seams and a shallow crowned roof give the body more legible construction. Fresh Metal [side](captures/family_carriage_side.png), [three-quarter](captures/family_carriage_quarter.png) and [roof-off interior review](captures/family_carriage_interior.png) were inspected. The roof-off image is a temporary review cutaway; the actual candidate keeps its roof. Detail is still broad blockout geometry, especially glazing/door fit and the coachman's anatomy; no measured historical coach design or final material approval is implied.

## Period basis and limits

- The [British Library's 1808 *Ekka* catalog record](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/?f%5Blevel_ssi%5D%5B%5D=%5B32%5D+Fonds&f%5Brelated_subjects_ssim%5D%5B%5D=Animals&page=9&per_page=50&search_field=all_fields&sort=relevance) identifies a one-horse carriage. Its [1815–1820 pony ekka record](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/?f%5Blevel_ssi%5D%5B%5D=%5B40%5D+File&f%5Brelated_subjects_ssim%5D%5B%5D=Transport&per_page=20&sort=hierarchy) confirms this type before the game's setting. A [later British Library ekka photograph](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-003088846) describes a two-wheeled horse-drawn Indian cart, but is dated to the 1890s and is not direct 1857 evidence.
- The open goods body is a **design inference**, not a reconstruction of a specific 1857 horse cart. Period sources more clearly document ox-drawn goods carts, including an [1800–1850 Patna painting](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_1989-1225-0-16). It should remain a candidate until a closer horse-drawn goods-cart reference is found.
- Both bodies are deliberately plain wood/leather/iron studies. The current horse is a low-poly CC0 candidate, not a verified regional breed. Harness geometry, traces under load, wheel/ground contact, driver posture, turning clearance, suspension, and road handling have not been approved. The rope load is blockout geometry.

## Arjun boarding trial — 2026-09-24

The three isolated candidates now expose seat sockets and E interaction points through the existing world marker overlay: a coachman step and either side cabin door on the family carriage, driver and passenger on the ekka, and driver on the goods cart. Boarding uses the existing `mounted_vehicle` contract; F exits only when a ground and capsule clearance check succeeds. The family coachman blockout hides while Arjun drives and returns after exit. Driver W/S/A/D input animates wheels and horses with a bounded ground and forward obstruction probe. The seated driver pose reaches the reins without replacing or scaling Arjun's mesh.

`tools/horses/validate_cart_boarding.gd` exercised the actual player, family carriage passenger and driver boarding, exits, and driver boarding of the ekka and goods cart in an isolated floor trial; it printed `CART PLAYER BOARD/DISMOUNT/DRIVER: PASS | family, ekka, goods`. `tools/horses/capture_cart_boarding.gd` produced Forward+/Metal [driver](captures/cart_arjun_driver.png) and [passenger cutaway](captures/cart_arjun_passenger_cutaway.png) frames. The driver frame shows the pelvis near the coachman cushion and hands close to the reins. The passenger view is a roof/ceiling-off diagnostic and shows the player in the cabin, but the bench, leg, foot and door clearance need closer side views and normal-speed review. The exterior driver and cabin are still broad art blockouts.

These are trials, not live-world vehicle approval. Carts still lack a collision body, road placement, reliable turns on varied terrain, opening doors and entry/exit motion; point boarding currently moves Arjun directly to the socket. Forward ray checks alone do not prevent all body collisions. The reference screenshots supplied by the owner show compact white Q/E keycaps in dark rounded frames; the existing overlay already supplies a world circle and E keycard, but exact visual matching and screenshot review remain open. Do not place these as production driveable carts until road/obstacle collision, motion/contact and 8 GB performance have been checked.
